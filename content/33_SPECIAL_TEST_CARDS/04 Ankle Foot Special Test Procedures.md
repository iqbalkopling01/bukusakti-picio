## Ottawa Ankle/Foot Rules

Use validated rule after acute injury to decide need for radiography based on malleolar/midfoot pain plus specified bony tenderness or inability to bear weight. Use exact official rule rather than memory fragments; age/exclusion criteria matter.

## Anterior Drawer Ankle

Patient sitting/supine, ankle slight plantarflexion. Stabilize distal tibia, cup calcaneus and translate talus/foot anteriorly. Increased translation or soft endpoint vs side supports ATFL laxity. Acute pain/guarding limits reliability.

## Talar Tilt

Stabilize tibia and invert talus/calcaneus at ankle/hindfoot. Excess inversion/laxity can support CFL/lateral ligament involvement. Ensure movement is not only forefoot inversion.

## Squeeze Test Syndesmosis

Compress tibia and fibula together at mid/proximal calf. Distal syndesmosis pain can support high ankle sprain. Pain at compression site is less meaningful.

## External Rotation Stress

Stabilize lower leg, knee flexed, ankle neutral/dorsiflexed according protocol, externally rotate foot relative tibia. Distal anterior syndesmosis pain supports syndesmotic injury.

## Thompson Test

Patient prone with feet beyond table, squeeze calf. Normal response plantarflexion. Absent markedly reduced plantarflexion suggests Achilles rupture. Compare side; partial tear can be less clear.

## Windlass Test

Extend great toe in weight-bearing or seated position, tensioning plantar fascia. Familiar medial plantar heel pain may support plantar fascia involvement. Observe arch response.

## Navicular Drop

Measure navicular height in subtalar neutral then relaxed standing; difference estimates pronation-related change. Reliability depends landmark and positioning. Not diagnostic for pain by itself.

## Single-leg heel raise

Functional calf/Achilles test. Count full-height quality repetitions with knee extended and balance support standardized. Pain, low height or rapid fatigue can identify plantarflexor capacity deficit.

## Functional tests

Hop, lateral hop, figure-8 and change-of-direction are later-stage tools. Do not use simply because athlete is pain-free walking; ensure ROM, strength and basic balance prerequisite.
