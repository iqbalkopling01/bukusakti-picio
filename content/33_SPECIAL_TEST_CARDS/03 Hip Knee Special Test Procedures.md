## Hip FABER

Supine, place test leg figure-4: flexion-abduction-external rotation with ankle near opposite knee. Stabilize opposite ASIS and gently lower test knee. Note groin vs posterior/SI-region pain and range/asymmetry. Not structure-specific.

## FADIR

Flex hip/knee, then adduct and internally rotate. Familiar anterior/groin pain can support intra-articular/impingement-related hypothesis but high sensitivity/low specificity in many populations.

## Log Roll

Supine leg relaxed, gently roll entire limb IR/ER. Excess rotation/asymmetry or familiar deep hip pain may indicate hip joint irritability/laxity.

## Hip Scour

Hip flexed/adducted, apply axial load through femur while moving arc. Familiar deep joint pain/click can suggest intra-articular involvement. Avoid aggressive load in suspected fracture/acute OA flare.

## Thomas Test

Patient holds opposite hip flexed to flatten lumbar/pelvis; test thigh allowed toward table. Thigh staying flexed suggests hip flexor length limitation; knee position helps interpret rectus femoris. Pelvic control is essential.

## Trendelenburg

Single-leg stance. Observe contralateral pelvic drop or trunk compensation. Indicates inability to maintain pelvic control under hip abductor demand, not isolated gluteus medius diagnosis.

# Knee

## Lachman

Supine knee ~20–30° flexion. Stabilize distal femur, grasp proximal tibia and translate anteriorly. Assess excursion and end-feel vs other side. Increased translation/soft endpoint supports ACL insufficiency. Hamstring guarding can mask result.

## Anterior Drawer

Knee 90°, foot stabilized, pull proximal tibia anterior. ACL assessment but less sensitive in acute phase due hamstrings/position. Check posterior sag first because PCL injury can create false appearance.

## Posterior Sag

Hip/knee flexed ~90°, observe tibial plateau from side. Posterior drop relative to femur suggests PCL insufficiency.

## Posterior Drawer

From 90° knee flexion, push tibia posterior. Increased translation/soft endpoint supports PCL injury.

## Valgus Stress

Apply valgus force at 30° flexion to bias MCL, and near 0° to assess broader capsuloligamentous involvement. Compare pain/laxity.

## Varus Stress

Analogous lateral stress for LCL/lateral structures at 30° and 0°.

## McMurray

Flex knee, rotate tibia and apply varus/valgus while extending through range. Joint-line pain/click may support meniscal lesion. Use gentle controlled motion.

## Thessaly

Patient standing with support, knee slight flexion, rotate body/knee internally/externally. Joint-line pain/locking may support meniscus. Avoid in acute unstable/painful knee.

## Bulge/Stroke Test

For small effusion, milk fluid from medial side superiorly then stroke lateral side downward and observe medial fluid wave/bulge.

## Patellar Ballottement

For larger effusion, compress suprapatellar pouch and gently press patella posteriorly; floating/tap sensation suggests fluid. Avoid excessive force.

## Patellar Apprehension

With knee slight flexion, gently translate patella laterally while observing apprehension. Positive fear/apprehension supports instability history more than pain alone.
