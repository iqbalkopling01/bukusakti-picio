## Cozen test

Untuk lateral elbow extensor tendon pain. Elbow sekitar 90°, forearm pronated, patient makes fist and extends/radially deviates wrist against resistance. Familiar lateral epicondyle pain = positive pattern.

## Mill test

Passive stretch extensor group: elbow extended, forearm pronated, wrist/fingers flexed while shoulder position controlled. Familiar lateral epicondyle pain supports extensor tendon irritability. Do not over-stretch acute tissue.

## Maudsley / resisted middle-finger extension

Resist middle-finger extension with elbow extended/forearm supported. Lateral elbow pain may load extensor digitorum/radial tunnel region. Not diagnostic alone.

## Valgus stress elbow

Test UCL/medial stability. Stabilize humerus, apply valgus force at elbow in slight flexion to unlock olecranon. Pain/laxity compared side. Acute trauma needs fracture screen first.

## Moving Valgus Stress

Shoulder abducted/external rotated, elbow flexed; maintain valgus torque while moving elbow through arc. Familiar medial pain in throwing athlete can support UCL involvement. Needs trained handling.

## Tinel cubital tunnel

Tap gently over ulnar nerve behind medial epicondyle. Familiar paresthesia into ulnar digits may be positive. Strong tapping can cause symptoms in normals.

## Phalen

Maximal comfortable wrist flexion with dorsal hands together or protocol variant for up to standard duration. Median-territory paresthesia supports carpal tunnel. Stop early if symptoms reproduce.

## Reverse Phalen

Wrist extension palms together. Similar goal with different tunnel loading.

## Durkan / Carpal Compression

Apply direct pressure over carpal tunnel/median nerve for standardized period. Reproduction of familiar median paresthesia. Combine with sensory/motor findings.

## Tinel carpal tunnel

Tap over volar carpal tunnel. Paresthesia median distribution may support local nerve irritability.

## Froment sign

Patient holds paper between thumb and index using key pinch. Pull paper. Flexion of thumb IP via FPL compensation suggests adductor pollicis/ulnar weakness. This is motor sign, not pain test.

## Finkelstein

Therapist grasps thumb and performs ulnar deviation/stretch of first dorsal compartment carefully. Familiar radial styloid pain supports De Quervain. Often more controlled than Eichhoff.

## Eichhoff

Patient places thumb in fist then ulnar deviates wrist. Can be more provocative and generate false-positive pain; use cautiously.

## Thumb CMC Grind

Axial compression and rotation of first metacarpal at CMC. Familiar base-of-thumb pain/crepitus can support CMC OA but provocation may be uncomfortable.

## Tendon integrity hand

FDP: hold other fingers extended, ask DIP flexion of target finger. FDS: hold non-tested fingers extended, ask PIP flexion. Extensor tendon assessed through active extension and specific joint patterns. Tendon rupture requires referral, not repeated resisted testing.
