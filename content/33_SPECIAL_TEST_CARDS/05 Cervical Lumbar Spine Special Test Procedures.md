# Cervical

## Spurling

Patient seated. Position cervical extension, side-bending/rotation toward symptomatic side depending variant, then controlled axial compression. Positive when familiar radicular arm pain/paresthesia reproduced, not local neck pain alone. Avoid with instability, severe osteoporosis, myelopathy concern or acute trauma.

## Cervical Distraction

Patient supine/seated, apply gentle axial distraction. Reduction of familiar arm symptoms can support nerve-root compression hypothesis. Dizziness/nausea/neurologic worsening = stop.

## ULNT

See neurodynamic card. Median-biased ULNT commonly combines shoulder depression/abduction/external rotation, forearm supination, wrist/finger extension and elbow extension. Structural differentiation through cervical side-bend or release component. Exact sequence follows lab protocol.

## Hoffmann / Clonus / Gait

Not mechanical radiculopathy tests. Positive UMN signs plus hand clumsiness, bilateral symptoms or gait imbalance raise cervical myelopathy concern and need medical evaluation.

# Lumbar

## Straight Leg Raise

Supine, passively flex hip with knee extended. Record angle and symptom distribution. Radicular pain below knee/familiar neural symptom often more meaningful than posterior thigh tightness. Ankle dorsiflexion or hip adduction can increase neural load; reducing hip flexion should reduce.

## Crossed SLR

Raise asymptomatic leg; reproduction of symptomatic contralateral radicular pain is less sensitive but can be more specific for disc-related root irritation.

## Slump

Sitting slump thoracic/lumbar and cervical flexion, extend knee and dorsiflex ankle. Structural differentiation by releasing cervical flexion or ankle. Do not aggressively perform in highly irritable nerve.

## Femoral Nerve Stretch / Prone Knee Bend

Prone, flex knee and possibly extend hip to load upper lumbar/femoral neural structures. Anterior thigh neural pain, not just quadriceps stretch, is relevant. Screen hip and local muscle.

## Repeated Movement / Centralization

Repeat flexion/extension or directional movement in standing/lying while monitoring symptom location. Centralization of distal symptom can guide exercise in selected patients. Peripheralization or neurologic worsening changes plan.

## Lumbar Quadrant/Kemp-like provocation

Extension/side-bend/rotation loads posterior elements/foramen but is nonspecific. Use only to reproduce comparable sign and with safety.

## SI provocation cluster

Distraction, compression, thigh thrust, sacral thrust and Gaenslen are used as pain provocation cluster. Multiple concordant positives are more useful than one test; exclude lumbar/hip first.

## Red flags before special test

Trauma/fracture risk, cauda equina, progressive motor deficit, infection, malignancy, abdominal/vascular symptom or severe unremitting pain override mechanical testing.
