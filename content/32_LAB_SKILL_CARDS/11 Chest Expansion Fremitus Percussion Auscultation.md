## Chest expansion by palpation

Posterior lower chest: place hands around lower ribs with thumbs near midline and small skin fold between thumbs. Ask deep breath; observe thumb separation and symmetry. Upper chest can be assessed near upper thorax. Compare sides, not only total excursion.

## Chest expansion by tape

Select and mark level, commonly axilla, xiphoid or lower/subcostal depending protocol. Tape horizontal, snug not compressing. Record at maximal expiration then maximal inspiration. Repeat consistently. Pain, poor effort and cognition affect measurement.

## Fremitus

Use same phrase and voice volume. Move hands symmetrically from upper to lower chest. Consolidated lung transmits vibration more strongly; pleural air/fluid can reduce. Obesity/muscle thickness also changes intensity, therefore asymmetry is often more useful.

## Percussion sequence

Percuss intercostal spaces, side-to-side. Posterior avoid scapula, anterior account heart/liver dullness. Dullness over one basal region can reflect fluid or consolidation; interpret with breath sounds/fremitus. Hyperresonance with reduced breath sounds and acute dyspnea raises pneumothorax concern.

## Auscultation map

Posterior: apices above/near scapular region then interscapular to bases. Lateral: axillary fields. Anterior: upper lobes and right middle/lingula regions as anatomically accessible. Listen directly on skin.

Normal categories commonly described as vesicular over peripheral lung and bronchial/tracheal centrally. Adventitious sounds require location, timing, change after cough and symptom context.

## Example reasoning bronchopneumonia

Alveolar inflammatory exudate → density increases → percussion can become dull and fremitus may increase. Secretions → crackles/rhonchi and productive cough. Reduced compliance/pain → lower expansion and rapid shallow breathing. The Drive case explicitly uses this chain to interpret physical findings.

## Example reasoning pneumothorax

Pleural air → sound transmission decreases and percussion becomes hyperresonant; chest expansion may decrease on affected side. Acute tension physiology with distress/hemodynamic compromise is emergency, not physiotherapy assessment exercise.
