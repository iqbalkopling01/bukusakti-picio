## 1. Inspection

Sebelum menyentuh pasien, lihat consciousness, ability to speak, color, respiratory rate, depth, rhythm, chest symmetry, nasal flaring/retraction, accessory muscle, cough, sputum, oxygen device, posture and mobility. Count RR tanpa membuat pasien sengaja mengubah napas bila mungkin.

Patterns: tachypnea, bradypnea, rapid shallow breathing, prolonged expiration, paradoxical movement. Forward-lean posture dapat muncul pada dyspnea obstructive. Clubbing/edema memberi clue chronic systemic/cardiopulmonary condition tetapi tidak spesifik.

## 2. Palpation

Assess local temperature/tenderness, chest wall movement, accessory muscle activity and tactile fremitus. Place hands symmetrically and compare rather than relying absolute intensity.

## 3. Chest expansion

Measure with hands or tape at standardized level. With tape, record circumference at maximal inspiration and expiration; difference is expansion. Keep tape level and tension identical. Drive CABG material describes axillary, xiphoid and subcostal regions. Use same landmark serially.

## 4. Tactile fremitus

Place ulnar/palmar edge of hands at symmetric chest locations while patient repeats same phrase. Increased transmission can occur over consolidation; reduced over pleural fluid/pneumothorax or thick tissue. Compare sides and map region.

## 5. Percussion

Hyperextend middle finger of non-dominant hand firmly on intercostal space, strike distal phalanx with opposite middle finger using wrist snap. Compare side-to-side at equivalent level. Resonant = aerated lung; dull = denser tissue/consolidation/fluid; hyperresonant = excess air. Avoid scapula/bone and do not overinterpret one note.

## 6. Auscultation

Use diaphragm of stethoscope directly on skin. Ask slightly deeper mouth breathing if tolerated. Compare symmetrical fields from apices to bases anterior/posterior/lateral. Listen full inspiratory/expiratory cycle. Document location and phase.

Crackles: discontinuous popping, often fluid/opening small airways. Wheeze: musical continuous sound from narrowed airway. Rhonchi/low-pitched coarse sounds can relate larger airway secretions and may change after cough. Reduced/absent sounds can indicate poor ventilation, effusion/pneumothorax or technical issue.

## 7. Cough/sputum

Ask whether cough is dry/productive, spontaneous/assisted, painful, and whether sputum is easy to expectorate. Observe huff/cough force. Color alone does not diagnose organism.

## 8. Functional challenge

If stable, assess sit-to-stand/walking and record HR, SpO2, RR, Borg and recovery. Many patients are limited by deconditioning even after breath sounds improve.
