## Bed mobility

Assess rolling, bridging, scooting and supine-to-sit. Record assistance: independent, supervision, contact guard, minimal/moderate/maximal assistance or local terminology. Note whether limitation is strength, pain, trunk control, cognition or line/device.

## Sit-to-stand

Standardize chair height, arm use and foot position. Observe forward trunk lean, symmetrical weight shift, knee control and need for upper limb. Progress by reducing arm support, raising/lowering seat, adding repetitions/load and changing speed only after safety.

## Stand-pivot transfer

Position destination close and at appropriate angle. Lock wheelchair/bed equipment. Manage foot placement and device. Guard at pelvis/trunk rather than pulling upper limb. In hemiplegia or post-op, respect affected arm/weight-bearing restriction.

## Sliding-board transfer

Used when lower-limb weight bearing or force is insufficient but upper limb/trunk can assist. Ensure skin protection, board placement and incremental scooting. Avoid shear on fragile skin.

## Gait device selection

Cane: modest unloading/balance, generally contralateral hand to affected lower limb for mechanical advantage unless reason otherwise. Crutches: greater unloading but require UE strength/balance. Walker: larger base and support but can reduce speed and requires handling skill. Rollator allows continuous gait but brakes/cognition matter.

## Cane height

Commonly handgrip near wrist crease/greater trochanter region with elbow slight flexion when standing upright, but fit to patient and device. Do not set by formula alone if posture/contracture differs.

## Walker sequence

For reduced weight-bearing limb: advance walker, affected limb, then stronger limb according to prescribed WB. For reciprocal walker/rollator, pattern may differ. Follow weight-bearing restriction exactly.

## Stairs with cane/crutch

Classic teaching: up with stronger limb first, down with device/affected limb first, when non-reciprocal and medically appropriate. Once capacity allows, reciprocal stairs should be trained if goal requires.

## Outcome

Document device, distance, assistance, speed, turns, fatigue and safety. "Walks with walker" is incomplete documentation.
