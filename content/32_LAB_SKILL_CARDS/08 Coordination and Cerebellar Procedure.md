## Finger-to-nose

Patient alternates touching own nose and examiner target. Change target position. Observe dysmetria, intention tremor and decomposition. Compare sides and control for vision.

## Finger-to-finger/finger chase

Patient follows moving examiner finger. Helps expose timing and endpoint control.

## Rapid alternating movement

Rapid pronation-supination on thighs or alternating hand. Observe speed, rhythm and amplitude. Dysdiadochokinesia suggests coordination deficit but weakness/apraxia can also impair performance.

## Heel-to-shin

Supine, place heel on opposite knee then slide down shin and back. Observe trajectory and smoothness. Weakness or sensory loss can mimic cerebellar ataxia.

## Rebound/check

Use caution. Test ability to stop movement when resistance suddenly released only when safe. Modern clinical use is less common; avoid striking patient.

## Truncal and gait ataxia

Assess sitting without support, standing base, tandem stance/gait and turning. Cerebellar gait often wide-based with variable steps; sensory ataxia may worsen markedly with visual removal.

## Differentiate

Cerebellar: dysmetria, intention tremor, ataxia despite vision. Sensory: impaired proprioception, worse with eyes closed. Vestibular: vertigo/nystagmus and directional imbalance. Frontal/apraxic gait has different initiation pattern.
