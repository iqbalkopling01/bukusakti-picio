## Vesicular

Soft low-pitched normal sound over peripheral lung with inspiration longer/louder than expiration.

## Bronchial

Louder/high pitch with expiration prominent, normal over trachea. If heard peripherally may suggest consolidation but clinical context essential.

## Crackles

Discontinuous popping. Fine crackles may relate to opening small airways/interstitial process; coarse can accompany secretion. Ask whether changes after cough.

## Wheeze

Continuous musical sound from narrowed airway. Polyphonic widespread wheeze common in obstructive airway narrowing.

## Rhonchi

Low-pitched continuous sound often associated larger airway secretion and may change after cough.

## Reduced/absent breath sound

Can occur with shallow breathing, pleural effusion, pneumothorax, severe obstruction, obesity/chest wall, or technical position. Compare side-to-side.

## Breathing patterns

Tachypnea: increased rate.

Bradypnea: decreased rate.

Apnea: absence breath.

Prolonged expiration: obstructive clue.

Accessory muscle use: increased work breathing.

Paradoxical breathing: segment moves opposite expected; can indicate severe dysfunction depending context.

Orthopnea: dyspnea lying flat, often cardiac/respiratory clue.

## PT documentation

“Fine inspiratory crackles bilateral bases, unchanged after huff” is more useful than “abnormal sound”.
