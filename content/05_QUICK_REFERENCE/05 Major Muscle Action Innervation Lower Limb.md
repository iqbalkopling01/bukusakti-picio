## Hip

Iliopsoas: hip flexion. Femoral/ventral rami L1–L3 depending component.

Gluteus maximus: hip extension/external rotation. Inferior gluteal L5–S2.

Gluteus medius/minimus: abduction and frontal-plane pelvic control. Superior gluteal L4–S1.

TFL: flexion/abduction/internal rotation contribution. Superior gluteal.

Adductors: hip adduction. Obturator mainly L2–L4.

Piriformis/deep rotators: external rotation in certain positions, stabilization. Nerves from sacral plexus.

## Knee

Quadriceps: knee extension. Femoral L2–L4, mainly L3–L4 clinically.

Hamstrings: knee flexion and hip extension except short head biceps. Sciatic L5–S2.

Popliteus: unlock knee/rotational control. Tibial nerve.

## Ankle

Tibialis anterior: dorsiflexion/inversion. Deep fibular L4–L5.

EHL: great toe extension/dorsiflex contribution. Deep fibular L5.

EDL: toe extension/dorsiflexion. Deep fibular L5–S1.

Gastrocnemius: plantarflexion and knee flexion. Tibial S1–S2.

Soleus: plantarflexion/postural control. Tibial S1–S2.

Tibialis posterior: plantarflexion/inversion, arch support. Tibial L4–L5.

Fibularis longus/brevis: eversion/plantarflex contribution. Superficial fibular L5–S1.

## Functional shortcut

Hip abductor weakness → pelvic drop/Duchenne compensation.

Quadriceps weakness → difficulty sit-to-stand/stair, possible knee instability.

Dorsiflexor weakness → foot drop/steppage.

Plantarflexor weakness → reduced push-off and gait speed.

Hip extensor weakness → difficulty rise/stair/running acceleration.
