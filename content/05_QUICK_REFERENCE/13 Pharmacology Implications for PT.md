## Why PT should care

Medication changes heart rate, blood pressure, alertness, balance, pain, bleeding, muscle performance and exercise response. PT does not prescribe these drugs but must recognize implications.

## Beta blockers

Can blunt HR response to exercise. Use RPE/symptoms/workload rather than age-predicted HR alone.

## Antihypertensive/diuretic

Orthostatic hypotension, dehydration or electrolyte issue can influence balance/exercise. Monitor BP and dizziness.

## Anticoagulant/antiplatelet

Higher bruising/bleeding risk. Be cautious with aggressive manual techniques and fall/trauma.

## Corticosteroids systemic long-term

Bone loss, muscle weakness, skin fragility, infection risk possible.

## Analgesic

Pain reduction may allow function but can mask overload. Ask timing/dose changes.

## Sedative/psychotropic

Drowsiness, reaction time, orthostasis, fall risk may change.

## Levodopa/dopaminergic Parkinson meds

Performance varies by on/off period. Document medication timing when testing gait/balance.

## Bronchodilator

May improve airflow; some cause tremor/tachycardia. Coordinate respiratory treatment relative to medication as prescribed.

## Insulin/glucose lowering

Exercise affects glucose. Patients at risk hypoglycemia need education/monitoring per medical plan.

## Practical medication history

Name, purpose, dose schedule, recent changes, side effects perceived. Never tell patient to stop prescription medication independently.
