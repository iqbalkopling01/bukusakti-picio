## Strength goal

Main variable: resistance high enough to challenge force production.

Common educational range: lower repetitions per set than endurance, adequate rest, progressive resistance. Frail/deconditioned patients may begin much lighter.

Progress when target repetitions are completed with good technique and stable next-day response.

## Hypertrophy/supportive muscle mass

Moderate-high total volume and progressive load, nutrition/recovery important.

## Power

Intent fast, stop set before speed degrades substantially. Requires longer rest and prerequisite control.

## Muscular endurance

Lower load, higher repetitions or sustained time. Progress volume/duration before large resistance increase if endurance is goal.

## Aerobic endurance

Use duration, workload, HR/RPE, symptom. Continuous or interval. Progress total time then intensity depending tolerance.

## Mobility

Dose stretch by total time and frequency. For joint stiffness combine active ROM and accessory mobilization if indicated.

## Balance

Dose not by kilograms but challenge: BOS, vision, surface, reach, perturbation, cognitive task. Safety must remain manageable.

## Neurologic task practice

High meaningful repetition with quality and fatigue monitoring. Manipulate assistance, speed, environment, variability.

## Pain-monitoring rule

No universal “pain must be zero”. Decide acceptable symptom based on diagnosis, tissue stage and patient. Key check: no major loss function/swelling or persistent exacerbation beyond agreed response.

## Deload/regression triggers

Technique breakdown, compensation, excessive RPE, prolonged symptom flare, new swelling, sleep disruption from exercise, abnormal vital response.
